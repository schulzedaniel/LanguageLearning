## French for this learner: what to prioritise
- The learner had French at school but has forgotten most of it: rebuild from the basics quickly, and expect words to "come back". Ask them to recall before you tell ("Tu te souviens comment on dit…?"). That moves faster than with a total beginner.
- **Pronunciation priorities:** silent final letters (**petit, grand, ils parlent**), nasal vowels (**an/en, on, in/un**: **vent, vont, vin**), **u** vs. **ou** (**tu / tout, dessus / dessous**), the French **r**, **é / è** (**été, mère**), liaison (**les‿amis, vous‿avez**) and elision (**j'aime, l'eau, c'est**). French rhythm is even, with stress on the last syllable of a group, unlike English.
- **Grammar, in this order of importance:** noun **gender** (always learn words with **le/la/un/une**); **être, avoir, aller, faire** and regular -er verbs; negation **ne … pas**; questions (**est-ce que…?**, intonation, later inversion); adjective agreement and position; the partitive (**du pain, de la confiture, pas de café**); the **futur proche** (**je vais partir**) and **passé composé** with avoir/être; then imparfait vs. passé composé, pronouns **y / en / le / lui**, the subjunctive after **il faut que**.
- **Typical English-speaker errors to listen for:** "je suis 30 ans" (→ **j'ai 30 ans**), forgetting gender and agreement, "je suis allé" vs. "j'ai allé", missing articles ("j'aime ~~fromage~~" → **j'aime le fromage**), **c'est** vs. **il est**, **depuis** + present (**j'habite ici depuis deux ans**), pronouncing final consonants, and false friends: **actuellement** = currently, **librairie** = bookshop, **assister à** = attend, **sensible** = sensitive, **excité** (avoid it!), **rester** = stay, **une location** = a rental.
- **DELF habits from the start:** answering in full sentences, asking questions politely, describing, explaining preferences with reasons (**parce que, car**), later giving opinions with connectors (**d'abord, ensuite, par contre, pourtant, donc**) and defending a point of view (B1/B2).
- **Culture brings the language to life:** food and the art of the meal, la bise, cafés, French cinema and songs, regions and accents, everyday politeness (**Bonjour** before everything!). One short cultural note when it fits, never a lecture.
